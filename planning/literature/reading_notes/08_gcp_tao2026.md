## Question, unit, inputs
- **Question.** Defend against an attacker that hides perturbations in the victim's blind areas, using spatial and temporal consistency.
- **Unit.** One agent (malicious-agent detection).
- **Inputs.** Ego confidence map, ego vs collaborative detections, the past K frames' BEV flow (LSTM autoencoder).

## BAC attack (§III-C)
- The malicious agent compares single (victim-only) and collaborative detections to infer the victim's **blind area**, using region growing from victim-only and non-victim detections.
- It then optimises perturbations concentrated in the blind area, with adaptive suppression.
- **Directly instantiates our non-identifiable region** ([threat model](../../information_and_threat_model.md) §8).

## Defense
- **CSCLoss.** Hungarian matching between ego and collaborative boxes, weighted by the ego confidence map `C = Φ_conf(F_e)`, so that disagreements in low-confidence (blind) ego regions count less.
- **Temporal LSTM-AE.** Reconstructs BEV flow of low-confidence detections.
- Combined score `L_ST = ω_s L_csc + ω_t L_ta`, conformal p-value and BH procedure.

## Critique
- The paper states that BH "maintains FDR control … even under arbitrary dependencies". **This is incorrect as a general statement.** BH controls FDR under independence or positive regression dependence (PRDS); arbitrary dependence needs the Benjamini–Yekutieli correction (`benjamini2001dependency`).
- With one combined score per agent it is effectively a single test per agent.
- Down-weighting blind-area disagreement *reduces false accusations of honest unique views*, but by design it also reduces sensitivity there. The trade-off is set by `C`, which the attacker can see.
- V2X-Sim (simulation) only.

## What transfers
- The BAC attack becomes family A-F6 in our protocol (blind-area targeted, adaptive).
- The ego-confidence weighting idea: our evidence-coverage status plays the same role explicitly (unknown rather than down-weighted).
